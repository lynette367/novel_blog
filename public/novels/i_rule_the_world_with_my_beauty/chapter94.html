Xue Yuan ran out of the capital city when the sky was dazed, and when he passed the first stagecoach station on his way, he was stopped by the people who greeted him at the gate of the stagecoach station.

These people took his horses and prepared hot water and hot dishes. A fine room, a soft bed, excellent medicinal herbs, and a respectful doctor and attentive servants waiting to heal Xue Yuan's wounds.

After Xue Yuan had a good night's rest, his horse was brought out early the next morning, with smooth fur, wearing a beautiful and delicate harness, with a loud snort, and with a sufficient amount of fresh water and dried meat already prepared on its back, and in the same general spirit as its owner.

Xue Yuan mounted his horse in wonder, and again galloped toward the northern frontier. But he was treated so generally and properly at every post he passed, and sometimes when he did not make it to the post, the people in the post would even take stoves and seasonings with them and travel to him in the wild mountains to make him a fragrant meal in the wilderness.

After three times, Xue Yuan understood that this was the emperor's reward.

Xue Yuan kindly laughed, his chest trembling low.

This was his Imperial Majesty telling him that even though Gu Yuanbai's body was weak, his hands and feet were cold, and he was a master who would fall ill at the slightest moment of inattention, His Imperial Majesty was still firmly in the position of the superior, and he could use his monstrous power to grant Xue Yuan comfort along the way.

Such a reward hardened Xue Yuan as if he were a consort in the Emperor's harem, needing to be pampered and receiving strong favor from His Holiness.

It is true that it is the minister who enters the arms of the Son of Heaven, not the Son of Heaven who enters the arms of the minister.

Such behavior would undoubtedly prolong Xue Yuan's time to catch up, but Xue Yuan still took the emperor's arrangements one by one.

The most weary heart was melted into water.
